The working rules for keeping the personal information in Semivra Libellus
safe. It is for the owner, managers and the Data Protection Officer (DPO). The
customer and staff notices tell people what we do; this says how.

## 1. The Data Protection Officer

The Business designates a DPO (NPC Advisory 2017-01). In a small business this
is usually the owner or a trusted manager. The DPO:

- answers requests from customers and staff about their information;
- keeps this manual, the notices and the processing records up to date;
- leads the response to any breach (section 6);
- is the contact for the National Privacy Commission.

## 2. What we hold, and why

| Records | Personal information | Why | Keep for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orders and receipts | Customer name, phone, delivery address, table | To fill and deliver the order; to support the sale in the books | As long as tax law requires the books to be kept [confirm with accountant] |
| Senior citizen / PWD discounts | Name and OSCA / PWD ID number | Required to grant and document the statutory discount (RA 9994, RA 10754, BIR rules) | Same as the sale |
| Client accounts | Contact details, registered name and address, TIN, credit terms and balance | To sell on account and collect | While the account is active; then as long as the tax records it supports |
| Staff | Name, role, login, hashed password/PIN, clock and shift records, pay, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG/TIN | Employment, payroll, statutory contributions, cash accountability | At least 3 years after the last entry; longer where tax law requires |
| Suppliers | Contact person, phone, email, address, TIN | Purchasing and paying | While active; then as long as the tax records it supports |
| Audit trail and sessions | Who did what, when, from which device | Security and accountability | [set a period, e.g. 3 years] |

**Sensitive personal information** (government ID numbers, and the fact that a
customer is a PWD) is collected only because the law requires it, is shown
only to people who need it, and is never used for anything else.

## 3. Who may see what

Access follows the system's permissions. Rules for the owner:

- Give each person their **own login**. Never share one.
- Give the **narrowest role** that lets them work. The system lets you narrow a
  role down to single pages (Admin Panel → Access Roles).
- Only payroll and accounting roles should see staff pay and government
  numbers.
- Switch a login off **the day someone leaves**.
- Review who has access **every quarter**.

## 4. Everyday rules

- Lock the screen or switch user when you step away from the till.
- Do not photograph, copy or print customer or staff information except for the
  order or task in front of you.
- Printed receipts and reports with personal information are shredded, not
  binned.
- Exports and backups (Ledger → Export All) contain personal information: keep
  them on a password-protected device, never in a shared chat or public drive,
  and delete them when no longer needed.
- Counter tablets that show the ordering QR without a sign-in ("Just QR") are
  turned on by a manager, one by one, and switched off in Settings → QR display
  when a tablet is replaced or lost.

## 5. Requests from customers and staff

Anyone may ask to see, correct, delete or take a copy of their information, or
object to its use. When a request comes in:

1. Confirm who is asking (ask for something only they would know, or ID in
   person). Do not send information to an unverified email or number.
2. Log it: date, who, what they asked.
3. Answer within **15 working days**. Use the system's exports for copies; edit
   the record for corrections.
4. Deleting: remove what the Business has no legal duty to keep. Where tax or
   labour law requires the record (a sale, a payslip), explain that it is kept
   for the legal period and then deleted.
5. Record what was done.

## 6. When something goes wrong (a breach)

A breach is any loss, theft, or access to personal information by someone who
should not have it - a lost tablet that was signed in, a leaked export, a
wrong person given a login, a report of misuse.

1. **Contain it at once.** Switch off the affected logins (Admin Panel), switch
   off lost QR tablets (Settings → QR display), change passwords, and tell
   Semivra if the system itself may be involved.
2. **Assess it.** What information, whose, how many people, and could it cause
   them harm (identity theft, fraud, embarrassment)?
3. **Notify within 72 hours** when sensitive personal information or
   information that could enable identity fraud is involved and there is a real
   risk of serious harm: notify the **National Privacy Commission** and the
   **affected people** (NPC Circular 16-03). Semivra will support the
   notification.
4. **Record everything** - even breaches that did not need notifying - and
   include them in the annual report to the NPC if the Business is registered.
5. **Fix the cause**, and update this manual.

## 7. Registration with the NPC

Registration of the Business's data processing systems is **mandatory** when
it employs **250 or more people**, or processes the **sensitive personal
information of 1,000 or more individuals**, or its processing is otherwise
likely to pose a risk to people's rights (NPC Circular 2022-04 - [confirm the
current thresholds with the NPC or counsel]). A business below the thresholds
is not required to register, but **the rest of the Data Privacy Act still
applies in full**: the notices, the DPO, security, and breach notification.

Because every senior citizen and PWD discount records an ID number, a busy
shop can pass 1,000 such customers sooner than expected. Check the count once a
year.

## 8. Review

Review this manual, the notices and the list of sub-processors **once a year**,
and whenever the business starts collecting something new.
